Penguins in your DOM

remaking xpenguins & xsnow (1999) as a web engine — demo page

This page looks like a blog post on purpose: it is the test bench for web-penguins. Every box, image, heading and code block you see is detected by the engine as a window — the same concept the 1999 originals used on X11 desktops. Penguins parachute in from the top of your screen, land on whatever they land on, and get on with their lives.

Try things: click a penguin (sorry). Scroll away and come back — they're still there, going about their business. The whole page is their world: they stay alive on and off screen, and the bottom of the page is their desktop floor. Let the snow pile up on the cards below; it stays where it fell.

This is a card. Penguins treat it as a solid window: they walk along its top edge, climb its sides, and occasionally sit down up there to read a book. Snow settles on it too.

The original behaviour, ported line by line

The walking, climbing, tumbling, floating, splatting and exploding all come from xpenguins_core.c — same probabilities, same 8-pixel step height, same terminal velocity. The snow physics and Santa are from xsnow. The sprites are the original XPM pixmaps, converted losslessly to PNG.

<script src="webpenguins.js" data-auto
        data-penguins="25" data-trees="3"></script>

// or programmatically:
const flock = WebPenguins.start({ penguins: 25, snow: true });
await flock.stop();          // the classic bomber exit sequence

Things penguins can do to your layout

Nothing, actually. The whole show is one <canvas> overlay with pointer-events: none; your page never knows it's there. The engine only reads the DOM to find surfaces.

Pixel-perfect collisions

The word below is a transparent <canvas>. The engine reads its alpha channel and builds a collision mask, so penguins stand on the letterforms themselves — not the bounding box — tumble into the gaps between letters, climb the tall stems, and snow settles on the glyph tops. The original xpenguins pulled the same trick with the X11 SHAPE extension for shaped windows.

Long paragraphs are deliberately boring so that the boxes stand out. The engine ignores plain text: penguins won't balance on a comma. They will however happily walk on headings, tables, buttons and anything that looks like a card — border, shadow, or its own background.
